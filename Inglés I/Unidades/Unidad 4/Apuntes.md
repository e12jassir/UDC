# Unidad 4 — Future Plans and Goals
**Inglés I · ENGV001-E2 · Modalidad Virtual**

---

## 🧭 Unit Overview

This unit focuses on **expressing the future** in English using *will* and *going to*, plus **modal verbs** for obligation, ability, possibility, and advice. You will be able to talk about your plans, goals, dreams, and intentions.

**Core Topics:**
- Future with *will* (predictions, spontaneous decisions, promises)
- Future with *going to* (plans, intentions, predictions with evidence)
- Modal verbs: can/could, must/have to, should, may/might
- Vocabulary: future plans, goals, life events, education and career

---

## 1. Future with *Will*

### 1.1 Structure

| Form | Structure |
|:---|:---|
| **Affirmative** | Subject + **will** + base verb |
| **Negative** | Subject + **will not (won't)** + base verb |
| **Interrogative** | **Will** + subject + base verb + ? |
| **Short answers** | Yes, I will. / No, I won't. |

```
I will graduate in 2029.
She won't miss the deadline.
Will you join the project?  → Yes, I will. / No, I won't.
```

### 1.2 When to Use *Will*

| Use | Description | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Predictions** (opinions) | About the future, often with *think, believe, probably* | I think AI will change the world. |
| **Spontaneous decisions** | Decided at the moment of speaking | "The phone is ringing." → "I'll answer it!" |
| **Promises** | Commitment to do something | I promise I will study harder. |
| **Offers** | Offering to do something | I'll help you with the project. |
| **Refusals** | *won't* = strong refusal | She won't change her mind. |

## 2. Future with *Going To*

### 2.1 Structure

| Form | Structure |
|:---|:---|
| **Affirmative** | Subject + **am/is/are going to** + base verb |
| **Negative** | Subject + **am/is/are not going to** + base verb |
| **Interrogative** | **Am/Is/Are** + subject + **going to** + base verb + ? |

```
I am going to apply for a scholarship next year.
He isn't going to miss the exam.
Are they going to submit the project on time?
```

### 2.2 When to Use *Going To*

| Use | Description | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Pre-made plans** | Decision was made before the moment of speaking | I'm going to study computer science. |
| **Intentions** | Strong intention or desire | She's going to start learning Python. |
| **Predictions (evidence)** | Future event based on visible evidence now | Look at those clouds — it's going to rain. |

---

## 3. Will vs. Going To — Comparison Table

| Situation | Will | Going To |
|:---|:---:|:---:|
| Plan made in advance | ❌ | ✅ |
| Spontaneous decision at the moment | ✅ | ❌ |
| Promise | ✅ | ❌ |
| Prediction based on evidence | ❌ | ✅ |
| General prediction / opinion | ✅ | ✅ |

**Examples of the same situation:**
```
(Phone rings) → "I'll get it!"          (spontaneous → will)
(Bought tickets) → "I'm going to see the concert." (plan → going to)
```

> ⚠️ **Error común:** Usar *will* para todos los casos futuros. La distinción planificado vs. espontáneo es importante en inglés.

## 4. Modal Verbs

### 4.1 Overview Table

| Modal | Main Meaning | Negative | Example |
|:---:|:---|:---|:---|
| **can** | Ability / Permission | can't | I can code in Python. |
| **could** | Past ability / Polite request | couldn't | Could you help me? |
| **must** | Strong obligation (internal) | must not (mustn't) | I must finish the assignment. |
| **have to** | Obligation (external rules) | don't have to | I have to attend class. |
| **should** | Advice / Recommendation | shouldn't | You should practice every day. |
| **may** | Formal permission / Possibility | may not | May I leave early? |
| **might** | Weak possibility | might not | I might apply for the internship. |
| **would** | Polite requests / Conditionals | wouldn't | Would you mind helping? |

> **Key distinction: must vs. have to**
> - *Must*: the obligation comes from **the speaker's own feelings**
>   - "I must study — I want to pass."
> - *Have to*: the obligation comes from **an external rule or authority**
>   - "I have to submit the form by Friday." (the professor requires it)

### 4.2 Must Not vs. Don't Have To

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **mustn't** | Prohibition — it is forbidden | You mustn't plagiarize your work. |
| **don't have to** | No obligation — it's optional | You don't have to submit it early. |

> ⚠️ These are very different! "You mustn't" = you cannot do it. "You don't have to" = it's optional, up to you.

### 4.3 Modal Verb Structure

`Subject + modal + base verb (no -s, no -ed, no -to)`

```
✅ She can speak English.
❌ She can speaks English.
❌ She can to speak English.
❌ She must studied.
```

### 4.4 Degrees of Possibility (Epistemic Modals)

```
100%: will      → "It will rain tomorrow." (certain)
90%:  must      → "She must be home; the lights are on." (logical deduction)
50%:  may       → "It may snow tonight." (possible)
30%:  might     → "He might come to the party." (less likely)
0%:   can't     → "That can't be right." (impossible)
```

## 6. Speaking — Talking About Plans (Sample Dialogue)

```
A: What are you going to do after you graduate?
B: I'm going to work as a software developer. What about you?
A: I think I'll travel first. Maybe I'll go to Canada for a year.
B: That sounds amazing! Will you study English there?
A: Yes, I might take some courses. But I haven't decided yet.
B: You should apply soon. Visas can take a long time.
A: You're right. I must start the application process next month.
```

## 7. Writing — Goals and Plans Essay Model

**Task:** Write a short paragraph about your future plans and goals using *will*, *going to*, and modal verbs.

**Model text:**
> I have clear goals for my professional future. I am going to graduate from the Software Engineering program at UDC in 2029. After graduation, I will look for a job as a junior developer in a tech company. I would also like to do an internship before I graduate because it can provide valuable real-world experience. I might study English in another country for a short period to improve my communication skills. In five years, I think I will be working as a full-stack developer, and I may start my own project. My main goal is to build technology that can help people in Colombia. I must keep studying and practicing every day to achieve this.

## 8. Errores Comunes de Hispanohablantes

| Error | Incorrecto | Correcto | Explicación |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| *Will* para planes previos | *"Tomorrow I will go to the dentist"* (plan agendado) | *"Tomorrow I'm going to the dentist"* | Planes previos → *going to* |
| Modal + infinitivo con *to* | *"She must to study"* | *"She must study"* | Los modales no llevan *to* (excepto *have to*, *ought to*) |
| Modal con -s o -ed | *"He cans"*, *"She musted"* | *"He can"*, *"She had to"* | Los modales son invariables |
| Confundir mustn't y don't have to | *"You mustn't worry"* (cuando es opcional) | *"You don't have to worry"* | *Mustn't* = prohibición, *don't have to* = opcional |
| Usar *will* para certeza 100% basada en evidencia | *"Look at those clouds, it will rain"* | *"It's going to rain"* | Con evidencia visible → *going to* |
| *Would* = *quería* (pasado) | Confundir con condicional | *Would* en inglés es condicional/petición cortés, no solo pasado | |

## 11. Quick Reference

```
WILL:
(+) Subject + will + base verb
(-) Subject + won't + base verb
(?) Will + subject + base verb?
USE: spontaneous decisions, promises, predictions (opinions)

GOING TO:
(+) Subject + am/is/are going to + base verb
(-) Subject + am/is/are not going to + base verb
(?) Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + base verb?
USE: pre-made plans, intentions, evidence-based predictions

MODAL VERBS (never + to, never + -s):
can/could: ability, permission
must: strong obligation (self-imposed)
have to: obligation (external rule)
should: advice
may/might: possibility (may > might in certainty)
would: polite requests, conditionals

MUST NOT vs. DON'T HAVE TO:
mustn't    = FORBIDDEN (no puedes hacerlo)
don't have to = OPTIONAL (no tienes que hacerlo, pero puedes)
```
